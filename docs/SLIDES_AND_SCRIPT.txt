SLIDE 5  —  THE META TRAP (and our honest fix)                 (1:30)
================================================================================
ON SLIDE:
- Meta reports NO revenue — only a "conversion" count (~1.66 million)
- That count is NOT purchases
- Naive fix (conversions × price) = ~$146M of FAKE sales
- Our fix: Meta revenue = spend × real return rate (Google+Bing, ~4.75×) ≈ $930K
- Clearly labelled as an estimate
IMAGE: 04_meta_fix.png
SAY:
"Meta gives us no revenue, only a 'conversion' count — and that count adds up to about
1.66 million, which is clearly not real purchases. Now, the naive thing to do would be to
multiply that count by an average order value. If you do that, you invent about a hundred
and forty-six MILLION dollars of fake sales — it would completely swamp everything else and
make the whole forecast meaningless. Instead, we estimate Meta's revenue as its spend times
the REAL return rate we actually see on Google and Bing — roughly four-point-seven-five
times — which gives a sensible figure around nine hundred and thirty thousand dollars. And
we clearly label it as an estimate everywhere it appears. I'm highlighting this because it
shows we didn't just run a model — we actually looked at the data and handled its biggest
trap honestly. Many teams will have missed this."

SLIDE 7  —  HOW A FORECAST IS MADE (4 steps)                   (1:30)
================================================================================
ON SLIDE:
1. Tidy up — merge 3 messy files into one clean table
2. Make clues — summarise each campaign's recent behaviour ("features")
3. Predict a range — low / middle / high sales for 30-60-90 days
4. Explain — AI writes a plain briefing (what, why, risks, actions)
IMAGE: 02_pipeline_steps.png
SAY:
"Under the hood, the tool does four things in order. Step one, tidy up: it reads the three
messy files and merges them into one clean table, fixing the units and estimating Meta's
revenue along the way. Step two, make clues: for each campaign, it summarises the recent
behaviour — how much it spent and earned lately, its trend, and the season — into numbers
the model can learn from. In machine learning we call these 'features'. Step three, predict
a range: a trained model looks at those clues and outputs a low, middle, and high sales
figure for each time window; ROAS is then just sales divided by spend. And step four,
explain: the AI reads the numbers and writes a short, plain-English briefing. Let me spend
a moment on step three, the model, because that's the technical heart of the project."

SLIDE 8  —  THE MODEL (in plain words)                         (2:00)
================================================================================
ON SLIDE:
- LightGBM — a fast, proven prediction method
- We train THREE: one each for the low, middle, high line (quantile regression)
- Then a "conformal" honesty check: widen the range if it was too narrow on past data
- Budget is an input to the model — that's why the budget simulator is instant
IMAGE: 06_coverage.png  (optional; or keep text-only)
SAY:
"We use a method called LightGBM — it's a fast, well-proven prediction algorithm that's
excellent at learning from tables of data. The clever part is that we don't train one model;
we train three. One learns the LOW line, one the MIDDLE, and one the HIGH line — this
technique is called quantile regression, and it's what lets us output a range instead of a
single number. But there's a catch: a raw model's range can be dishonest — too narrow, so
the truth falls outside it too often. So we add a calibration step called conformal
prediction. In plain terms: we test our low-to-high range on past data, and if it was too
narrow, we widen it until it's trustworthy. This gives us a mathematically honest range, not
just a guess. One more thing: the planned budget is one of the model's inputs — and that's
the reason the budget simulator you'll see in a moment reacts instantly, with no retraining."

BACK-POCKET ANSWERS (for Q&A — say briefly, confidently)
================================================================================
- "How do you handle Meta's missing revenue?"  ->  Estimate it as spend times the blended
  Google+Bing return rate (~4.75x), clearly labelled as an estimate; avoids a $146M error.
- "Why a range, not one number?"  ->  Business planning needs best and worst cases; a single
  number hides risk. Ours is calibrated to ~80% coverage (we measured 89–94%).
- "Will it run on our data?"  ->  Yes — one offline command, pinned versions, model committed,
  verified on a clean clone and 30 edge cases.
- "Where does the AI fit?"  ->  Only to explain our computed numbers — it never invents
  figures — and it's outside the scored path, so it can't break scoring.
- "What's the model?"  ->  Three LightGBM quantile models (low/middle/high) plus conformal
  calibration for honest ranges; campaign forecasts add up to channel and total.
- "What would you improve next?"  ->  Replace the Meta estimate with real Shopify/GA4 revenue;
  add holiday and promotion calendars for richer seasonality.
